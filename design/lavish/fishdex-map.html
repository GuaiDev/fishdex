Explore — the live spots map

Drag to pan, scroll to zoom, tap a marker, open Filters and Layers.

Who it's for: anyone deciding where to go. Spots are the pins at every zoom; the water body is the context around them. Density follows the contract's tier (headline spots first, more appearing as you zoom in); only what is in view is drawn, exactly as a bbox + tier call intends. The two new control surfaces — Filters and Layers — are the round's headline change.

Water bodies are drawn as muted lines from their own geometry; lakes with no outline get a label, not a shape. The chip row, map key and every card string come from GET /map/spots/labels and the spot payload. The two round icons open Filters and Layers. Tapping a marker fetches its detail on demand, with a loading state.
1
Filters are real params.

Species and Near-me map to species= and near=lat,lon,radius on GET /map/spots. Kind is the chip row. New-to-me is a disabled slot, marked phase 5 — the chip exists, the data does not.

2
Layers came off the spot.

Getting-there and Bridges used to be drawn from each spot's own arrays. They are now their own bbox endpoints, so a parking spot shared by two spots appears once. Bridges stay off by default.

3
Lakes: outline or honest label.

A curated lake gets its OHN Waterbody polygon matched by position. A lake with no match shows a name label and “no outline on file” — never an invented shape.

4
Weed edges are yours alone.

The private layer is on by default for you, off for everyone else. Every report is stamped reported, not verified; there are no counts and no sharing.

5
Conditions, with their source and age.

The pin sheet shows flow as a trend, weather, water temperature and chemistry — each with source, station, distance and age. A stale value is shown but flagged, never hidden.

6
Still quiet about scores.

Nothing colours water by a score. tier only decides what draws at which zoom, and the word never appears in the phone.

Filters and layers — the round's headline change

species=, near=, GET /map/layers/*.

The filter model (§5.4) is now backed by query parameters instead of a client-side pass. Everything the user can narrow by, and where it is served:

Lake outlines and shoreline features

GET /map/water-bodies + /map/water-bodies/{id}/features.

A curated lake's OHN Waterbody polygon is matched by position at build time, never by name alone, and served as geometry with its source OGF_IDs, name, effective date and licence on attributes. A lake with no match stays geometry: null — the honest answer on the map is a label, not a shape. Lake Ontario in this snapshot is exactly that case. Shoreline features (inlets, outlets, islands, points, peninsulas) are read off the lake's outline and the OHN streams that end at its shore.

illustrative — no matched outline in this fixture
Shoreline features
1
Inlet / outlet — a record.

A mapped OHN stream ends at the shore. Provenance record; derivation phrased “where a mapped stream meets the shore.” The Credit River inlet below is the doc's own example coordinate.

2
Island — a record.

A ring inside the source polygon. Carries area_m2. Derivation phrased “from the shoreline outline.”

3
Point / peninsula — an inference.

A shape computed from the outline's own edge. Provenance inference, with length_m for how far it reaches out. Marked as inference, never as a fact about fish.

4
No outline, no awkwardness.

Lake Ontario's own card shows geometry: null and the note “No outline is on file for this water, so it has no shape on the map yet.” The map draws its label and says so.

5
Deeper structure is not built.

Drop-offs, humps and weed lines need bathymetry, not an outline — see Coming soon. The only weed edges are the private ones a user reports.

The polygon and the point/peninsula markers on this demo map are drawn from the contract's own feature shape for review. No lake in the fixture has a matched outline (geometry: null throughout), so the live map shows labels only; against the live API the matched polygons draw beneath the spot pins.

Personal — the pin sheet, with conditions

PR #21 · GET /pins/{id}/conditions.

Switch the phone to Personal and tap a pin to open this sheet. Conditions arrive grouped — weather, water flow, water temperature, water quality — and each value carries its source, station, distance and age. A stale value (a single dated sample, not a live reading) is shown but flagged, never hidden. Flow and level come as a trend with the reasoning the API already wrote.

Personal pin — conditions panelRendered from the real pin_conditions_response.json (12 fields, four groups). The map behind is left un-drawn here so the sheet is the subject; in the live phone it is the same map as Explore, with your pins on top.
1
Every value has a source.

Weather from Open-Meteo; flow and level from WSC gauge 02HB027, 3 km away; temperature and chemistry from PWQMN, 1.4 km away. The station name is shown, not hidden.

2
Age, not freshness theatre.

“5 min ago” for weather; “60 min ago” for flow; “2157 days ago” for the single water sample. Age is the input, shown plainly.

3
Stale is shown, flagged.

Water temperature, DO and pH are each from one 2020 sample, so each carries a stale badge. Hiding them would read as “no data”; the badge says “old data”.

4
Trends come with the reasoning.

Pressure falling — “ahead of weather, often triggers a feeding window.” Flow rising, level steady. The API writes the meaning; the UI does not.

The spot card, still four blocks

card on GET /map/spots/{spot_id}.

Unchanged this round, and still rendered straight from a real fixture response by the same code the map sheet uses. Blocks 1–2 open, 3–4 expand. The hook changes shape with what the spot has: a plain structure sentence, an honest empty, or a protected-species care message that names no fish.

Fetch states — loading, not found, bad input

every fetch has all three

Tapping a marker fetches its detail on demand. The sheet shows a loading state first; a spot id that no longer exists returns 404 and the card says so plainly; a bad filter returns 422 with the API's own sentence. The map stays usable in every case.

Rules the UI keeps

Checked against every screen above.
Where each contract rule shows up.
RuleHow it is kept
A spot is one reason at one placePins are never merged by proximity; two reasons = two pins, each opening its own card.
Every word comes from the APIChips, kinds, block titles, species, citations, conditions labels and the “why fish gather here” copy are read from the payloads, never composed.
Lakes have a shape or a label, never a guessAn unmatched lake is geometry: null; the map draws a label and the note “No outline is on file…”.
Inference is marked as inferenceShoreline points and peninsulas show provenance inference; islands and inlets show record.
Weed edges are private and unverifiedLayer is visible_to: you; each report reads reported, not verified; no counts, no sharing, no export.
Stale conditions are shown and flaggedTemperature, DO and pH carry a stale badge; the value is never hidden.
A protected-species card names no protected fishThe hook becomes the care message; the withheld species appears in no list and no count. Other recorded fish may still be listed.
Quiet water is distinct and lastOwn hue, hollow dashed marker, chip listed after every other kind.
Bridges are off by defaultThe Layers popover starts with Bridges off; a crossing is described, never drawn as a spot.
Never show tier, no scorestier only decides what draws at which zoom; nothing colours water by a score or says “popular”.
Fish records ≠ angler pressureThe card's “You might find” is records within 2 km with its caveat; your pins count your own fish only, and Explore never reads them.
Personal pins are privateNo export, share or public counts on a pin card.

Served now — everything this round

Filters (species=, near=), the getting-there and bridges layers, lake outlines and shoreline features, the private weed-report CRUD, and the pin conditions panel. This mockup draws them from the real fixture.

Fixture breadth to widen

The snapshot still carries cards for only a few spots and records for two of them, so the Species filter and the fish-record card states are thin. One card per kind and per hook variant would let every state be reviewed from data.

Not yet in the fixture

No example responses are captured for species=, near=, /map/layers/getting-there, /map/layers/bridges or /map/water-bodies/{id}/features, and no lake has a matched outline. The UI is wired to the documented shapes and marked illustrative.

Still to come

“New to you” and “recorded more often here” hook text; quiet-water spots; lake depth; harbours/piers/breakwaters; the conditions-match indicator and Today sort.
